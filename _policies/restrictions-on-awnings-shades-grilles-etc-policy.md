## 1. Purpose

Rule 32 prohibits erecting air-conditioning condensers, extractor fans, awnings, shades, sunscreens, venetian blinds, bamboo chicks, window/door/balcony grilles "and other like items" without the **prior written approval** of the Management. This policy sets out how approval is sought and granted, and how unauthorised erections are handled.

## 2. The rule in practice

- **Prior** means before installation — not after. Anything fixed to the exterior of a unit (including within balconies, where visible) needs written approval first.
- The office maintains approved designs/patterns, colours, materials (non-corrosive) and fixing methods, available on request.
- Where grilles are approved, they must be fixed **within** the unit — not outside windows or on external walls.

## 3. Applying for approval

1. Resident submits a written application: item, location/elevation, dimensions, materials and finish, method of fixing, and supplier drawing or brochure where available.
2. The office checks against the approved design catalogue and the Building Structure bylaws (no overload/impairment of structure).
3. Outcome is issued **in writing** with any conditions; approved items must match the approval. Rejections state the reason.
4. Installations are spot-checked on site visits; findings are recorded (KL-SV/KL-IR).

## 4. Unauthorised erections

Where an item is found without approval on record:

1. Record the observation with photographs (visit finding or issue).
2. Issue the 14-day compliance notice (letters tool, template **erect-14**): either remove and make good, or apply for approval with the required particulars and accept any conditions.
3. If neither happens within 14 days, the Management Corporation may remove and rectify at the owner's cost and impose an administrative charge, per the [Compliance & Enforcement Policy](/policies/compliance-enforcement-policy/) — the notice says this in advance.

## 5. Appeals

Approval rejections, *erect-14* notices and any resulting charges all carry the standard 14-day written appeal clause — see the [Appeals Policy](/policies/appeals-policy/).
